The decisions log (artifact 6) gains a Status column after the id: `<state> <date> · <decider>`, in fixed English keywords whatever the log's language. States are `proposed`, `accepted`, `declined`, `deprecated` and `superseded by d-…` (written in the old row, in place; the new row's Why says `supersedes d-…`). The decider is a stable alias of a person (`h1`), an agent's session (`agent s-…`) or `found` (read from the code); a proposed row adds `decides: <alias>`. Only a person accepts or declines; an agent writes `accepted · agent s-…` only for what principle 15 leaves to it, and writes `proposed` to change a person's decision. Why may begin with `unconfirmed:` when no person gave the reason, and ends with `accepting: <cost, with its number>` when the decision has a cost of its own. There is no body file per decision: the long form stays with the document that owns it, else with the changelog entry that took the decision, cited in the row.

## Evidence

The architecture decision record literature (Nygard's template, MADR, the collection that gathers them) asks for a status, timestamps, supersession and both kinds of consequence. On this machine's carriers, read-only: two logs invented, independently, an open section for half-decided rows with states and a who-must-decide role; one writes a date inside the Why cell in most rows; supersession is written in prose in several shapes in the largest log; no carrier keeps a file per decision; negative consequences appear in about a tenth of one log's rows. An external team's practice marks a reconstructed rationale as unknown and lets only a person accept a proposed record. Since 0.0.27, agents write decision rows (principle 15, `decision-review`). Design and reasons: `meta/reviews/2026-10-05-decision-records.md` in the home.
