A knowledge card helps a design answer when the answer turns its claim into a concrete step of that design, not when it restates the claim: given the same concern written as a principle or as a mechanism, a reader judging the answer picks the mechanism, so more cards consulted does not make an answer preferred.

## Evidence

Three single-shot answers to one design question (no knowledge base; seven cards picked by title; full routing through the index) were judged blind by one person, problem by problem, over eight problems. The seven-card answer was preferred in 5, the answer without the knowledge base in 2 and the fully routed answer in 1. The judge stated the choices were by content. The fully routed answer carried the most concerns but phrased many as principles; the preferred answer phrased fewer concerns as steps (an action counts only once an independent confirmation arrives, and an unconfirmed one is cancelled after a timeout). Confounds: the judge had read the seven-card answer before the experiment, that answer came from the session holding the conversation, and the label chosen was the same one in three problems in four.
