# Proposal: a-review-turn-has-a-shape

A review turn asked of a human has a shape, and the shape is what makes it answerable: three to five short
blocks of prose rather than one long one; every option shown by a concrete case at the same fidelity; one more
answer that opens the next level of detail rather than a flat "tell me more", so the human can go deeper on the
one option they doubt without reopening all of them; a teach-back of what the answer will cause before an
expensive or irreversible step; and, where a process is about to run, its steps offered as a table before the
plan rather than described in prose. Stated by one human across many sessions and held in an assistant's local
memory on one machine, which is exactly the place a rule is reverted everywhere else — so it belongs in the
method's question rules or nowhere.

## Evidence

One human, one machine, held as a local memory and applied in interactive reviews over several sessions,
including a review of twelve decisions on 2026-10-15 where every turn used it. No measurement: what would settle
it is a count of answers that needed a follow-up question, with the shape and without. The method's own question
principle already asks for a concrete case per option and a price; the parts it does not carry are the block
count, the one answer that opens a tree of detail, the teach-back before a costly step, and the table of steps
before a plan. Those four are the proposal. Literature: none consulted.
