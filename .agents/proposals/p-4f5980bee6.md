A new command, `bundle.py decisions FILE`, checks a decisions log and runs in the carrier's gate and in `close`. It fails on a state, date or decider that does not parse, on `superseded by` with no target or one not in the log, on a superseding row without its `supersedes`, and on an agent's acceptance that supersedes a row decided by a person or with a blank decider. It warns on every proposed row (with its age and who must decide), every `unconfirmed:` reason, and a backticked path in Enforced in that names no file. It reads columns by position, so a log in another language keeps its headings, and it never reads a private folder. A migration command fills each existing row's Status as `accepted recorded <date>`, the date of the first commit that holds its id, with a blank decider that counts as a person; the update session maps supersessions written in prose, and leaves `accepted` when unsure.

## Evidence

Without a check the new format sits on rung 1, and principle 2 says a name cited enforces nothing, which a dead enforcer path is. A bulk conversion of the logs in an earlier release would date many rows to one day if the migration took the last commit rather than the first. An external team's practice runs a structural validator for its records in a pre-commit hook and in its pipeline.
