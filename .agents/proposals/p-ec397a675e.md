When answers are compared blind through an option picker, shuffling which answer sits behind each label is not enough: if the labels keep fixed positions, position and label are confounded, so positions must be counterbalanced across problems and the rate of each position reported beside the result.

## Evidence

In an eight-problem blind comparison of three answers, the arm behind each of three labels was shuffled per problem, but the labels always appeared in the same order. The middle label was chosen three times in four and the first never. The judge stated the choices were by content, and the design could not separate that from a position effect. In the same session, an option list that always put the recommended option first saw it taken three times in four, with the same ambiguity.
