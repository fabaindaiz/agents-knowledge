# Proposal: concurrency-past-the-bottleneck-adds-latency

More concurrency past the bottleneck adds latency instead of throughput, and the limit is often one the usual reading does not show: a database connection pool larger than a small multiple of the cores raises tail latency, and a container whose CPU quota is spread in per-CPU slices is throttled by its quota while its average use sits far below it, on kernels before a fix. Reading low average CPU as no CPU limit, or raising the pool, the usual answers, are wrong. It stops applying where the work waits on remote calls inside the held resource, or with no quota. The check: sweep the pool size under load against p99, and read the cgroup's throttled-period counters against p99.

## Evidence

A connection-pool library's wiki, *About Pool Sizing* (a demonstration where shrinking the pool cut latency by more than an order of magnitude); the kernel's CFS bandwidth documentation and a public engineering post on the throttling regression (2019).
