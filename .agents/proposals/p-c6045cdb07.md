# Proposal: reordering-a-float-sum-changes-it

Optimising or parallelising a sum changes the order of additions and so the result: naive summation error grows with n, pairwise summation with log n at about the same speed, compensated summation stays near constant at several times the arithmetic. The usual answer, "reordering is free", breaks tests that compare exactly and drifts results past a tolerance at large n. It stops applying at few terms or an error far below the tolerance. The check: sum in the optimised order against an exact or extended-precision sum at the real n, and make tests tolerant of order.

## Evidence

Higham, *The Accuracy of Floating Point Summation*, SIAM J. Sci. Comput. (1993) (ASSUMPTION: from memory, not fetched); an encyclopedia summary of pairwise summation.
