# Proposal: a-single-thread-baseline-before-more-cores

Scaling a computation across cores or machines is not evidence that it is fast: several systems that scaled to many cores lost to one well-written thread, and a cache-friendly order of the data or a different algorithm gained more than adding hardware, their preprocessing included. The usual answer, "parallelise it", is wrong until the baseline exists. It stops applying where the data does not fit one machine or the single thread is saturated and cannot be improved. The check: measure the single-thread time, report the cores needed to beat it, and compare cache-miss counters before and after reordering.

## Evidence

McSherry, Isard, Murray, *Scalability! But at what COST?* (HotOS 2015): on graph workloads a single thread matched or beat the parallel systems, and a space-filling-curve edge order cut its own time by more than half. Generalising beyond graph workloads is an inference.
