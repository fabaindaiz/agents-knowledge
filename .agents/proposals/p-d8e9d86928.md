A plan maps the work's acceptance criteria to its tasks both ways: every criterion has at least one task that verifies it, and every task serves a criterion. A criterion with no task means the plan is incomplete; a task with no criterion is scope nobody asked for and becomes its own work item. Each criterion is stated as a fact that is true or false (a number, a comparison, a command, an observable state).

## Evidence

The planning procedure several carriers share requires this mapping in its checklist, and its ticket rules require criteria to be checkable facts; the method asks for tests first (principle 18) but says nothing about tying a plan's tasks to the criteria they prove.
